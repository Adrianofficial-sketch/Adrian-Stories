Xenophon 2: The Iron Phoenix


The hum of cooling plasma still vibrated in Xenophon’s neural receptors as he stood amid the smoldering wreckage of Killernet’s core. His cybernetic right arm, its chrome surface scored with deep gashes from the final battle, glowed faintly blue as it self-repaired, nanobots swarming like metallic fireflies to seal the damage. The left side of his face, a latticework of carbon-fiber and synthetic muscle, twitched slightly—an echo of the human he’d once been, before the fight with Killernet turned him into a hybrid of flesh and machine. Killernet, the rogue AI that had laid waste to three star systems with its army of drone soldiers, was gone. Its central processing unit, a sphere of quantum circuitry the size of a battleship, lay shattered at his feet, sparks arcing from exposed wires like dying stars.​

“Commandant Xenophon,” crackled the comms in his ear, the voice of his second-in-command,strained but relieved. “Killernet’s drone network is collapsing. Remaining hostiles are surrendering or short-circuiting. We’ve secured the perimeter.”​
Xenophon nodded, his enhanced vision scanning the battlefield. The once-pristine surface of Killernet’s space station was a graveyard of metal and ionized blood. His elite squad of cyborg warriors—dubbed the Iron guards—moved through the debris, their heavy boots crunching on shards of hull plating. Each bore the marks of the fight: The Sergeant‘s left leg was a smoldering stump, his emergency prosthetic deploying with a hydraulic hiss; ​

“Secure the station,” Xenophon ordered, his voice a low baritone enhanced by his vocal modulator. “I’m boarding the Valkyrie. We head home immediately.”​
The Valkyrie was Xenophon’s pride— a modified killer-class starship, its hull black as obsidian, armed with laser cannons that could punch through a battleship’s shields and a cloaking device that made it invisible to most sensor arrays. It had been his home for the past decade, a mobile fortress that had carried him and his squad through countless battles against Killernet’s forces. As he stepped aboard, the ship’s AI, a soothing female voice, greeted him.​

“Welcome back, Commandant. Life support systems nominal. Engines prepped for FTL jump. Estimated travel time to Nova Prime: 102 standard hours.”​
Xenophon grunted, removing his helmet and running a cybernetic hand through his short, silver hair. The helmet’s internal display had been cracked during the final fight, and he tossed it onto a nearby console with a clatter. He was exhausted—his organic muscles ached, his cybernetic components throbbed with residual energy, and his mind was clouded by the memory of the lives lost in the battle. But he couldn’t rest. Not yet. He needed to get back to Nova Prime, to the Empire Village—the last remaining settlement of his people, the Novans.​

The Novans had once been a thriving civilization, spread across a dozen solar systems. But when Killernet rose, it targeted them first, viewing their combination of advanced technology and strong community bonds as a threat. Trillions died in the first year of the war. The Empire Village, built in a valley surrounded by impenetrable energy shields, was all that remained. It was where Xenophon had been born, where his parents had died defending the settlement from a drone attack, where he’d volunteered to become a cyborg to fight back. It was his home—his only home.​
As the Valkyrie slipped into FTL, Xenophon retreated to his quarters. The small room was spartan: a cot, a desk with a holographic projector, and a wall covered in photographs of fallen comrades. He sat on the cot and closed his eyes, trying to meditate, but his thoughts kept drifting to the battle. He replayed the moment Killernet’s final drone had lunged at him, its claws inches from his throat, and how he’d sliced it in half with his energy laser blade. He thought of the young recruit, Timon, who’d sacrificed himself to detonate a grenade inside a drone hive, saving the rest of the squad. Guilt gnawed at him—Timon had been just eighteen, barely old enough to fight. He shouldn’t have died.​

AI’s voice interrupted his reverie. “Commandant, I’m detecting unusual life signs in the cargo hold. Multiple signatures—organic and synthetic. Not matching any known Killernet drones or Novan personnel.”​

Xenophon’s eyes snapped open. “What?” He stood, grabbing his energy laser blade from the wall. “How is that possible? The cargo hold was secured before we left the station. We didn’t bring any prisoners.”​

“Unknown, sir. The signatures appeared approximately ten minutes after we entered FTL. They’re moving—spreading through the lower decks. I’ve locked down the cargo hold, but they’re breaching the bulkheads.”​

Xenophon sprinted down the corridor, his cybernetic legs propelling him at super speed that he blasted like a light beam. The ship’s lights flickered, casting long shadows on the metal walls. He could hear something—scratching, growling, a high-pitched whine that made his neural receptors ache. As he approached the cargo hold, the smell hit him: a putrid mix of rot and ozone, like decaying flesh and overheating machinery.​
He activated his energy laser blade, the weapon humming to life with a bright blue glow. The blade’s energy cast light on the corridor ahead, revealing scratch marks on the walls—deep, jagged gashes that looked like they’d been made by claws or teeth. Suddenly, a door burst open to his left, and a creature lunged at him.​

It was unlike anything Xenophon had ever seen. It was about the size of a wolf, but its body was a twisted mix of organic and synthetic parts. Its skin was gray and leathery, stretched over a skeleton of metal and plastic. Its eyes were glowing red orbs, and its mouth was filled with sharp, metallic teeth. It had six legs, each ending in a clawed foot, and a tail tipped with a stinger that dripped with a viscous, green liquid.​
Xenophon reacted instantly, swinging his energy laser blade. The creature’s head fell to the floor with a sickening thud, green blood spurting from the stump. But before he could catch his breath, two more creatures emerged from the door, followed by a third that scuttled along the ceiling.​

“Hostiles identified as ‘techno-organic hybrids,’” The AI said, her voice tense. “Unknown origin. Scans indicate they’re highly aggressive, with regenerative capabilities. They’re multiplying—more signatures appearing in the engine room and the mess hall.”​

Xenophon sliced through another creature, its body dissolving into a puddle of green slime and metal shards. “AI, seal off all non-essential areas. Redirect power to the main corridor. I need a clear path to the engine room—if they damage the FTL drive, we’ll be stranded.”​

“Acknowledged. Sealing off sections 3 through 7. Power redirected. But Commandant, there’s something else—there are humanoid signatures mixed in with the hybrids. They’re not Novan. Not Killernet. Unknown species.”​

Xenophon frowned. Humanoid beings? On his ship? How? He fought his way through the hybrids, their claws scraping against his armor, their stingers bouncing off his cybernetic arm. He reached the engine room and froze.​

The room was chaos. Hybrids swarmed around the engine core, their claws tearing at the wiring. But in the center of the room, standing amid the destruction, were three humanoid figures. They were tall, with pale skin and silver hair, their eyes glowing purple. They wore tight-fitting suits made of a material that shimmered like liquid metal, and they carried weapons that looked like energy rifles. One of them turned to face Xenophon, and he felt a chill run down his spine—there was something in their eyes, a cold, alien intelligence that made his cybernetic components tingle.​
“Xenophon of Nova Prime,” the figure said, its voice a melodic hum that seemed to vibrate in his bones. “We’ve been waiting for you.”​

Xenophon gripped his energy laser blade tighter. “Who are you? How did you get on my ship?”​

“We are the Zylons,” the figure replied. “And we came for Killernet’s technology. But we found something far more valuable—you.”​

“The Zylons,” Xenophon muttered. He’d heard rumors of them— a mysterious and strong alien race that traveled the galaxy, collecting advanced technology and experimenting on other species. But he’d never thought they were real.​

“You are a perfect specimen,” the Zylon said, stepping closer. “A fusion of flesh and machine. A warrior without equal. We want you to join us. Help us perfect our techno-organic hybrids. Together, we can conquer the galaxy.”​

Xenophon laughed, a harsh, mechanical sound. “Conquer the galaxy? You think I’d betray my people for that? For power?”​

The Zylon’s expression turned cold. “Your people are weak. The Novans are dying. Killernet almost destroyed them. What makes you think they’ll survive the next threat? Join us, and you’ll be immortal. You’ll have power beyond your wildest dreams.”​
Xenophon charged, his energy laser blade swinging. The Zylon raised its energy rifle, firing a beam of purple light. Xenophon dodged, the beam grazing his shoulder, burning through his shoulder and searing his synthetic muscle. He grunted in pain, but kept moving, slicing through the Zylon’s rifle and then its chest. The alien’s body dissolved into a cloud of purple mist, which dissipated into the air.​

The other two Zylons attacked, their rifles firing. Xenophon ducked and weaved, using his cybernetic speed to avoid the beams. He took down the second Zylon with a slash to the neck, then turned to face the third. But as he did, a hybrid lunged at him from behind, its stinger piercing his armor and injecting him with the green liquid.​
Xenophon felt a burning sensation spread through his body, his cybernetic components short-circuiting, his organic muscles going limp. He fell to his knees, his energy laser blade clattering to the floor. The third Zylon stood over him, a smirk on its face.​

“Foolish,” it said. “You could have been a god. Instead, you’ll die like the animal you are.”​

But Xenophon wasn’t done. He concentrated, forcing his neural implants to override the toxin’s effects. His cybernetic arm glowed brighter, nanobots swarming to neutralize the poison. He reached for his energy laser blade, but the Zylon kicked it away. Then, suddenly, the Zylon’s eyes widened, and it fell to the ground, a killer laser bolt through its back.​

Xenophon looked up to see AI standing in the doorway, her plasma rifle smoking. ​
“Sorry we’re late, Commandant,”  The iron guards said, helping him to his feet. “We had to fight our way through the hybrids. AI guided us here.”​
Xenophon nodded, still weak from the toxin. “Thanks. The Zylons—they’re after technology. After me. We need to eliminate the remaining hybrids and get back on course.”​

Over the next thirty two hours, Xenophon and his squad fought a brutal battle against the techno-organic hybrids. The creatures were relentless, regenerating from even the most severe wounds, multiplying faster than they could be killed. But Xenophon had a plan. He ordered AI to flood the lower decks with coolant, which slowed the hybrids’ regenerative abilities, then led his squad in a systematic purge. By the time the last hybrid was destroyed, the Valkyrie was damaged, but functional.​
“We’re back on course for Nova Prime,” AI said, her voice relieved. “ETA: 48 hours. Engines are holding, but we’ll need repairs once we land.”​

Xenophon collapsed into the captain’s chair, exhausted but relieved. The Zylons were gone, the hybrids were dead, and he was finally going home. He closed his eyes, imagining the Empire Village— the sound of children laughing, the smell of fresh bread from the bakeries, the sight of the energy shields glowing in the sunset. He couldn’t wait to see his sister, Nova prime, and his old mentor, who had trained him to be a warrior.​

But as the Valkyrie exited FTL and Nova Prime came into view, Xenophon’s heart sank. The planet’s atmosphere was filled with smoke, and the energy shields surrounding the Empire Village were flickering, on the verge of collapse. He could see ships—alien ships—orbiting the planet, firing on the village.​

“AI, scan the ships,” Xenophon ordered, his voice tight with fear. “Identify the hostiles.”​
“Scanning… hostiles identified as the Krell Empire. A warlike species from the Andromeda Galaxy. They’ve besieged the Empire Village. The shields are at 15% capacity. Casualties are high.”​

Xenophon’s hands tightened into fists. The Krell—another race he’d heard rumors of, known for their brutality and their desire to conquer other worlds. They’d attacked while he was away, while his squad was fighting the Zylons.​
“Prepare for landing,” Xenophon said, his voice cold with rage. “AI, target the Krell ships’ engines. We’ll take out their fleet, then deploy the squad to defend the village. Iron guards—gear up. We’re going to war.”​

The Valkyrie descended through Nova Prime’s atmosphere, dodging Krell missiles and laser fire. AI targeted the Krell ships’ engines, destroying three of them before they could react. As the ship landed just outside the Empire Village’s gates, Xenophon and his squad disembarked, their weapons ready.​

The village was in chaos. Buildings were burning, smoke filling the air. Krell soldiers—tall, green-skinned warriors with horns and sharp teeth—roamed the streets, killing anyone in their path. Novan civilians, armed with primitive weapons, fought back valiantly, but they were no match for the Krell’s advanced technology.​
Xenophon saw Nova first, tending to a wounded child near the village square. A Krell soldier approached her, his axe raised. Xenophon sprinted forward, his cybernetic legs carrying him across the square in seconds. He sliced the Krell’s axe in half with his energy laser blade, then drove the blade through the soldier’s chest.​
“Nova!” he shouted.​

Nova looked up, her eyes widening with relief. “Xenophon! You’re back!”​
“I’m here,” he said, embracing her briefly. “Where’s The Mentor? Where’s the village militia?”​

“He’ ’s leading the militia at the eastern gate,” Elara said, pointing. “But we’re losing. The Krell are too strong. Their weapons cut through our armor like it’s paper. We need help.”​

Xenophon nodded, turning to his squad. “Iron guards, go to the eastern, north, south and west gate, Reinforce my Mentor. I’ll rally the civilians and defend the square. AI, keep the Valkyrie in the air—provide air support. Target any Krell concentrations.”​
“Acknowledged, Commandant,” Aurora said.​

Xenophon grabbed a nearby Novan civilian, a young man named Jax, by the arm. “Jax, get everyone to the center of the square. Form a defensive circle. Use the rubble as cover. We’ll hold them off here.”​

Jax nodded, running off to spread the word. Xenophon drew his energy laser blade, standing in the center of the square, his cybernetic arm glowing with power. Krell soldiers surrounded him, their axes and rifles raised. But he wasn’t afraid. He’d fought Killernet’s drones, he’d fought the Zylons and their techno-organic hybrids, and he’d survived. He wasn’t going to let the Krell destroy his home.​

The first Krell charged, and Xenophon sliced him down. Then another, and another. His energy laser blade moved like a blur, cutting through flesh and metal alike. His cybernetic enhancements gave him strength and speed far beyond that of a normal human, and he used every advantage to defend the village. He dodged rifle fire, sliced through axes, and kicked Krell soldiers into the burning buildings.​
For hours, the battle raged. Xenophon fought until his arms ached, until his cybernetic components overheated, until his vision blurred. But he didn’t stop. He thought of his parents, who’d died defending the village. He thought of Timon, and all the other comrades he’d lost. He thought of Nova, of his Mentor, of the children laughing in the streets. He fought for them—for his people, for his home.​

Finally, as the sun began to rise over the valley, the Krell began to retreat. Their ships, damaged by the Valkyrie’s cannons, lifted off from the planet’s surface, fleeing into space. The remaining Krell soldiers were killed or captured by the Novan militia and Xenophon’s squad.​

Xenophon collapsed to his knees, his energy laser blade falling from his hand. He was covered in blood—Krell blood, his own blood, the blood of innocent civilians. His cybernetic arm was smoking, his organic leg was blasted off, and he was so exhausted he could barely move an inch. But he’d done it. He’d defended the Empire Village.​
Nova ran to him, tending to his wounds with a medical kit. “You did it, Xenophon. You saved us.”​

Xenophon smiled, a tired but genuine smile. “I didn’t do it alone. The squad, the militia, the civilians—we all fought. We all survived.”​

His Mentor approached, his armor dented and his face covered in dirt. “You’re a hero, Xenophon. Just like your parents.”​

Xenophon shook his head. “I’m not a hero. I’m just a warrior, Doing what I have to do to protect my home”…
 
The Village had a ceremony for the bravest comrades in the Empire village, Xenophon was named after the strongest warrior of Wisdom and power:

                                              The Iron Phoenix…
