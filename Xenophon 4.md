Xenophon 4: The Tournament and the silver bug warning


The echoes of war had barely faded when Empire Village braced itself for what came next.

For months, the village had rebuilt: walls strengthened, weapons sharpened, and every citizen trained to fight. The survivors of Xenophon’s brutal Tournament now formed the elite Guardsmen—hardened, fearless, and drilled to fight 20 hours a day. The Iron Guards patrolled day and night. Nova Prime tended to the wounded and studied enemy technology. And Xenophon stood watch, his gaze always fixed on the horizon, knowing peace was only a breath away from ending.

He had turned his people into a weapon.

Now they would find out if it was enough.

One quiet afternoon, a scout raced into the square, pale and urgent.

“Lord Xenophon—silver bugs. All along the western fields. They’re moving, and they’re not natural.”

Xenophon followed. Beneath his boots, tiny metallic insects crawled across the dirt, glinting like shards of moonlight. He caught one in his palm: it twitched, hummed, and burned his skin with a faint acidic heat.

“Tracker bots,” he snarled. “They mark our position, and rot our lands from below. This is no accident. This is a spy network.”

His mind flashed to the Lord of the Seven Universes—the cosmic ruler who had already sent warriors to crush them, jealous of his rising strength.

Xenophon studied the bugs until he found their weakness: a fragile organic core, vulnerable to sharp acid.

“Bring me the acidic vegetable spray,” he ordered. “We burn these things out before they call an army down on us.”

As the spray melted the silver bugs into black sludge, a low, distant tremble shook the ground.

The air went cold. The sky dimmed.

Every guard tensed. Every weapon clicked ready.

Xenophon stood tall, staring into the empty distance.

“They’re coming,” he said quietly. “All of them. The evolved Krell, the Zylons, even Killernet… and the Lord’s personal forces. They want to finish what they started.”

Nova Prime stepped beside him, her hands clenched.

“The Tournament made us strong. The Golden Guards are ready. But this will be the largest war we’ve ever faced.”

Xenophon’s eyes blazed.

“Then we give them a battle they will never forget. We will not just survive.

We will become unbeatable.”

The wind howled. The walls shook.

And the next great war for Empire Village began.

No sooner had the last silver tracker bot melted into sludge than the sky darkened.

A storm of enemy ships blotted out the sun: evolved Krell warships, Zylon liquid-metal cruisers, Killernet drones swarming like locusts, and at their head—warships forged by the Lord of the Seven Universes, glowing with cold, ancient magic that drained technology itself.

Empire Village shook.

“To the walls!” Xenophon roared.

His voice boomed across every tower, every street, every training yard.

The Guardsmen—the survivors of his brutal tournament—took their positions. Five-man squads moved as one, blades and rifles ready. These were not ordinary villagers anymore. They had fought each other, killed when forced, trained 20 hours a day until pain meant nothing. They were hard. They were unbroken.

The Iron Guards marched in formation, heavy armor thudding, cannons charging. Their job: hold the front line.

And hidden in the forests and hills, the Golden Guards—the thousand toughest survivors of Xenophon’s greater tournament—waited in silence. They were the blade in the dark, the surprise backup that no enemy knew existed.

Nova Prime stood beside Xenophon, her medical teams ready behind the lines. “Their magic is neutralizing our tech,” she warned. “Just like last time.”

Xenophon’s jaw tightened. The Lord’s warriors had come once before, erased much of their technology and power. But they had not erased the will of Empire Village.

“Good,” Xenophon said. “Then we fight with skill. With courage. With everything the Tournament forged into us.”

The first wave crashed into the walls.

Krell warriors charged, their bodies fused with Zylon metal, healing from every wound. Killernet drones sliced through the air. The Lord’s soldiers marched forward, magic crackling, disabling cannons, frying circuits.

For a moment, it looked like the village would fall.

Then Xenophon attacked.

He led the Iron Guards head-on, his blade cutting through hybrid soldiers like they were straw. Every Guardsmen fought like ten men. They did not flinch. They did not retreat. This was what they had trained for. This was why the tournament existed—not for sport, but for survival.

“Golden Guards! Now!” Xenophon Roared.

From the trees and shadows, the thousand elite warriors burst out.

They hit the enemy’s rear in a storm of steel and fury. The Krell and Zylons screamed, trapped between two forces. The Lord’s soldiers turned, confused—they had expected weak, broken villagers, not an army of living weapons.

The battle raged for months.

Dirt turned red. Fire burned across the fields. Enemy bodies piled high. But every time the Lord’s magic tried to break them, the villagers stood taller. The training. The pain. The tournament. It all flashes out now.

Xenophon fought his way to the front of the enemy line, facing the Lord’s finest warrior.

Magic clashed with steel.

Power clashed with will.

With a final roar, Xenophon drove his blade home.

The enemy champion fell.

Their magic wavered.

Their formation shattered.

One by one, the invaders broke and fled.

Krell retreated. Zylons vanished. Killernet drones fell from the sky.

The Lord of the Seven Universes’ army was defeated—again.



When the dust cleared, Empire Village stood.

Bloodied. Scarred. But unbroken.

The crowd cheered until their throats were raw.

Xenophon stood atop the wall, looking at his peole.

The tournament had not just made them soldiers.

It had made them immortal.

Nova Prime smiled at him. “They thought they could destroy us.”

Xenophon stared at the horizon, where more enemies would surely come.

“Let them come,” he said quietly.

“Empire Village does not lose wars.

We become stronger.”


Far beyond the reaches of Empire Village, in a realm untouched by mortal skies, stood the Citadel of the Seven Universes—an imposing fortress forged from condensed starlight and obsidian, rising from the heart of a swirling nebula. Here, the Lord of the Seven Universes ruled, his throne carved from the core of a dead black hole, his presence radiating a cold, unchallenged authority that made even the most fearsome cosmic beings tremble. For eons, he had maintained order through power and fear, crushing any who dared to defy his will, and never had he faced a humiliation like this.

The silence of his throne room was shattered by a bloodied, trembling messenger—one of his most loyal lieutenants, his armor torn, his flesh seared by starfire, his breath ragged as he stumbled before the throne. He fell to his knees, forehead pressed to the floor.

“M-My Lord,” he stammered, “the army is gone. All of it—Krell, Zylons, your magic warriors—slain by Xenophon and his villagers.”

The Lord fell silent, fingers tapping his throne. Then rage erupted: he snatched the messenger, roaring, “Peasants defeated my army?” The messenger gasped, “Xenophon has starfire—their courage is unbreakable.” The Lord crushed him, tossing his corpse aside.

He glared at the scrying pool, seeing Xenophon’s triumph. “Summon my strongest,” he barked. “March on Empire Village. Burn it, break Xenophon, and erase his glory. This time, he dies.”

His servants bowed, and the Lord smiled cruelly. “Enjoy your victory, Xenophon. It ends soon.”
